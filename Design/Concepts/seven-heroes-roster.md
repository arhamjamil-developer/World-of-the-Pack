Combat abilities are role pitches, not implemented or balanced mechanics. Keep Wolvy a flexible fighter and Bobo a slower protector so they do not feel interchangeable. Lulu heals; Momo controls an area through magic; Tilly uses gadgets. Pip's speed must have a clear role without making him an uncontrollable liability.

## Preserve these visual anchors

- Wolvy: blue cape, gold paw brooch, balanced silhouette.
- Elva: triangular green hood, leaf tiara, leaf bow.
- Lulu: cream/aqua colors, open rounded head silhouette, crescent wand.
- Bobo: widest body, acorn shield, coral/honey colors.
- Pip: smallest agile figure, long mint scarf, map satchel.
- Momo: tall drooping hat, lavender stars, star-tipped wand.
- Tilly: circular goggles, rounded backpack, yellow overalls.

Human and wolf forms share eye color, costume colors, emblem, and signature accessory. Human forms have human anatomy; wolf forms have a readable wolf muzzle, ears, tail, and paws. These are friendly bipedal wolf forms matching the approved gameplay art, not realistic quadruped wolves.

## Refinements before detailed sheets

1. Compare all seven as solid silhouettes at gameplay size. Hats, hoods, shields, scarves, and backpacks should remain distinct from the overhead camera.
2. Keep Elva's bow recognizable as a bow with a visible string and grip in her final sheet. The small lineup emphasizes its leaf shape and is not a construction reference.
3. Separate Momo's silver fur and taller hat from Wolvy's darker gray fur and exposed head. Keep Tilly's wolf muzzle distinctly wolf-like rather than fox-like.
4. Standardize human-to-wolf height, clothing fit, paw footwear, and accessory attachment. The concept lineup is illustrative, not measurement-accurate.
5. Test card portraits at their actual phone size. Do not depend only on color for identification.

## Character sheet specification — next stage

Create one sheet per character after choosing the final concept details. Each sheet should contain:

- Human form: neutral front, side, and back; one three-quarter identity pose.
- Wolf form: neutral front, side, and back; one matching three-quarter pose.
- Gameplay camera studies: overhead front/back/left/right readability, especially the north-facing back view seen while allies advance upward.
- Shared scale guide, named palette swatches, accessory close-ups, and clear hand/paw construction.
- Four expressions: happy, determined, surprised, and worried.
- A compact action study for that character's role, rather than an unverified sprite animation strip.

Character sheets are references. Animation-ready sprites still require consistent frame sizes, pivots, direction coverage, timing, and import settings.

## Relationship to spawning

The seven names identify roster choices; they do not limit the battlefield to seven units. Repeatable cards still summon additional copies of the selected unit type. Whether these are literal hero duplicates or unnamed pack members following a lead hero remains a narrative decision. Do not silently change the agreed repeat-spawn mechanic.

Human/wolf pairing establishes identity. Whether transformation changes combat abilities, occurs during a run, or is only a presentation choice still needs a separate gameplay decision. Do not assume fourteen independent card types.
